SAINTS

Saints, the Twelve Apostles and the first witnesses of the Church

This collection presents saints and holy people connected with Jesus and the beginnings of Christianity. It includes His family and friends, people who encountered Him, the Twelve Apostles, and other important witnesses of the early Church.

Dates from the first century are often uncertain. Where Scripture and reliable historical sources do not give a birth or death year, this page says unknown rather than presenting a guess as fact.

M
01 · First

St. Mary — The Blessed Virgin

Mary was the mother of Jesus. The Gospels remember her faith, humility, courage and presence at key moments in Christ’s life, including the Crucifixion.

Feast: 1 January (Mary, Mother of God); 15 August (Assumption)

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Not historically established. Catholic teaching celebrates her Assumption; traditions differ on the details of her earthly end.

Why remembered: Her “yes” to God and her unique role as the mother of Jesus (Luke 1:26–38).

J
02 · Second

St. Joseph

Joseph was the husband of Mary and the earthly guardian of Jesus. A carpenter or craftsman by tradition, he protected and cared for the Holy Family.

Feast: 19 March; St. Joseph the Worker — 1 May

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; Christian tradition generally holds that he died before Jesus began His public ministry.

Why remembered: Faithfulness, humility, obedience and care for the Holy Family (Matthew 1–2).

J
03

St. John the Baptist

A prophet and preacher who called people to repentance and baptised Jesus in the Jordan River. He prepared the way for Christ.

Feast: 24 June (Nativity); 29 August (Passion)

Life: Late first century BC–about AD 28–30 (approximate).

Death: About AD 28–30; executed under Herod Antipas.

Why remembered: He recognised Jesus as the one who was to come and courageously spoke the truth (Mark 1:1–11; Matthew 14:1–12).

M
04

St. Martha of Bethany

Martha was the sister of Mary and Lazarus. She welcomed Jesus into her home and made a profound confession of faith before Lazarus was raised.

Feast: 29 July (with Mary and Lazarus)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Hospitality, practical service and her confession that Jesus is the Messiah (Luke 10:38–42; John 11:1–27).

M
05

St. Mary of Bethany

Martha’s sister and Lazarus’s sister. Mary sat at Jesus’ feet to listen to His teaching and anointed Him before His Passion in the Gospel account.

Feast: 29 July (with Martha and Lazarus)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Devotion to Christ, attentive discipleship and an act of love before His death (Luke 10:38–42; John 12:1–8).

L
06

St. Lazarus of Bethany

Lazarus was the brother of Martha and Mary. Jesus raised him from the dead, a sign that points to Christ as “the resurrection and the life”.

Feast: 29 July (with Martha and Mary)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: The Gospels do not record the year or circumstances of his later death.

Why remembered: His raising from the dead and the faith expressed by his sisters (John 11:1–44).

M
07

St. Mary Magdalene

A devoted follower of Jesus who witnessed the Crucifixion and was the first witness of the risen Christ in John’s Gospel. Jesus sent her to tell the disciples.

Feast: 22 July

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; later accounts are traditions, not securely established history.

Why remembered: Faithful discipleship and proclaiming the Resurrection (John 20:1–18).

E
08

St. Elizabeth

Elizabeth was the wife of Zechariah and the mother of John the Baptist. She welcomed Mary and recognised the blessing of the child Mary carried.

Feast: 5 November in the Roman Martyrology

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Her faith, gratitude and greeting to Mary (Luke 1:5–25, 39–45).

Z
09

St. Zechariah

Zechariah was a priest and the father of John the Baptist. The angel Gabriel announced that Elizabeth would bear a son; Zechariah later praised God.

Feast: 5 November in the Roman Martyrology

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; later stories about his death are not established by the Gospels.

Why remembered: His priestly service and the prophetic Benedictus (Luke 1:5–25, 57–79).

S
10

St. Simeon

A righteous and devout man in Jerusalem who, guided by the Holy Spirit, recognised the infant Jesus in the Temple and praised God.

Feast: 3 February in some Western calendars; Simeon the God-receiver is commemorated on 3 February in the Byzantine tradition.

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: His recognition of Jesus as God’s salvation (Luke 2:25–35).

A
11

St. Anna the Prophetess

An elderly widow and prophetess who worshipped in the Temple. She gave thanks to God and spoke about the child Jesus to those awaiting redemption.

Feast: 3 February in some Eastern Christian calendars

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Prayer, perseverance and recognising the Messiah (Luke 2:36–38).

N
12

St. Nicodemus

A Pharisee and member of the Jewish council who came to Jesus by night, later defended fair treatment for Him, and helped prepare His body for burial.

Feast: 31 August (Roman Martyrology, with Joseph of Arimathea)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: His search for truth and his eventual public service at Jesus’ burial (John 3:1–21; 7:50–52; 19:39–42).

J
13

St. Joseph of Arimathea

A respected council member and secret disciple of Jesus who courageously asked Pilate for Jesus’ body and provided a tomb for His burial.

Feast: 31 August in the current Roman Martyrology

Life: First century AD; from Arimathea; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Courage and reverence in caring for Jesus after the Crucifixion (Matthew 27:57–60; John 19:38–42).

J
14

St. Joanna

Joanna was the wife of Chuza, an official in Herod’s household. She supported Jesus’ ministry and was among the women who went to the tomb.

Feast: 24 May in the Roman Martyrology; 27 June in some Eastern calendars

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Generosity, faithful service and witness to the Resurrection (Luke 8:1–3; 24:1–10).

S
15

St. Susanna

Susanna is named among the women who travelled with Jesus and the Twelve, helping to support them from their resources.

Feast: No universally observed individual feast day is established in the Roman calendar.

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Quiet generosity and support for Jesus’ ministry (Luke 8:1–3).

C
16

St. Cleopas

Cleopas was one of the two disciples who met the risen Jesus on the road to Emmaus. They recognised Him when He broke the bread.

Feast: 25 September

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; no reliable historical year is recorded.

Why remembered: His encounter with the risen Christ and recognition of Him in the breaking of bread (Luke 24:13–35).

The foundation of the Church

The Twelve Apostles

The Twelve were chosen by Jesus as His closest apostolic witnesses. After Judas Iscariot’s death, Matthias was chosen by the gathered disciples to take his place (Acts 1:15–26). The list below therefore names Matthias as the twelfth Apostle.

St. Peter
01 · Apostle

St. Peter (Simon Peter)

A Galilean fisherman and brother of Andrew, Peter became a leading disciple of Jesus. He confessed Jesus as the Messiah, witnessed the Transfiguration, denied Jesus during the Passion, and was restored by the risen Christ. In Acts he became a principal preacher of the Resurrection and a leader in the early Church.

Feast: 29 June, with St. Paul

Life: First century BC/AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Traditionally martyred in Rome under Nero, often dated c. AD 64–67; the exact date is uncertain.

Remembered for: Leadership, preaching and the witness of martyrdom.

Scripture: Matthew 16:13–19; Luke 22:54–62; John 21:15–19; Acts 2.

St. Andrew
02 · Apostle

St. Andrew

Andrew was Simon Peter’s brother and a fisherman. In John’s Gospel he is among the first to follow Jesus; he then brought Peter to meet Him. The Gospels also show Andrew helping bring the boy with loaves and fish to Jesus and introducing Greek visitors who wished to see Him.

Feast: 30 November

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Traditionally martyred by crucifixion at Patras in Greece; the details and date are uncertain.

Remembered for: Bringing others to Christ and missionary witness.

Scripture: John 1:35–42; 6:8–9; 12:20–22; Mark 1:16–18.

St. James
03 · Apostle

St. James the Greater

James, son of Zebedee and brother of John, was a Galilean fisherman called by Jesus. He belonged to the inner group who witnessed the raising of Jairus’s daughter, the Transfiguration and Jesus’ agony in Gethsemane. Acts records that Herod Agrippa I had him killed by the sword.

Feast: 25 July

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Martyred in Jerusalem about AD 44 (Acts 12:1–2).

Remembered for: Being the first of the Twelve whose martyrdom is recorded in the New Testament.

Scripture: Mark 1:19–20; 5:37; 9:2–8; Acts 12:1–2.

St. John
04 · Apostle

St. John the Apostle

John, son of Zebedee and brother of James, was one of Jesus’ closest disciples. He witnessed key events in Jesus’ ministry and stood near the Cross. The Gospel tradition associates him with the beloved disciple; Christian tradition also identifies him with the author of the Gospel of John and the letters bearing that name.

Feast: 27 December

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Traditionally believed to have died at an old age in Ephesus; this is not dated securely.

Remembered for: His witness to Christ, teaching about divine love and the Fourth Gospel.

Scripture: Mark 1:19–20; John 19:25–27; 1 John 4:7–12.

St. Philip
05 · Apostle

St. Philip

Philip came from Bethsaida and was called directly by Jesus. He brought Nathanael to meet Christ, asked Jesus to show the disciples the Father, and helped Andrew bring Greek visitors to Jesus. He should not be confused with Philip the Evangelist mentioned in Acts.

Feast: 3 May, with St. James the Less

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later traditions describe missionary work and martyrdom, but the details are uncertain.

Remembered for: Inviting others to “come and see” Jesus.

Scripture: John 1:43–46; 6:5–7; 12:20–22; 14:8–11.

St. Bartholomew
06 · Apostle

St. Bartholomew

Bartholomew is named among the Twelve in the Synoptic Gospels and Acts. Many Christian traditions identify him with Nathanael, whom Philip brought to Jesus in John’s Gospel, although the Bible does not explicitly state that they are the same person.

Feast: 24 August

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later traditions differ about his missionary journeys and martyrdom; no precise account is historically certain.

Remembered for: Apostolic witness and the tradition of missionary preaching.

Scripture: Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:16–19; John 1:45–49; Acts 1:13.

St. Thomas
07 · Apostle

St. Thomas the Apostle

Thomas, also called Didymus, is remembered for his honest questions and his desire to see the risen Jesus. When Jesus appeared and invited him to examine His wounds, Thomas made the confession, “My Lord and my God!” Ancient Christian tradition connects Thomas with the spread of Christianity in India.

Feast: 3 July

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Traditionally martyred in India; the precise circumstances and date are uncertain.

Remembered for: His confession of the risen Christ and missionary tradition in India.

Scripture: John 11:16; 14:5; 20:24–29; 21:2.

St. Matthew
08 · Apostle

St. Matthew

Matthew was a tax collector called by Jesus to become a disciple. The Gospel according to Matthew is traditionally attributed to him and presents Jesus as the fulfilment of Israel’s Scriptures. The New Testament does not provide a detailed biography of his later missionary life.

Feast: 21 September

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later traditions differ about where and how he died; details are uncertain.

Remembered for: Leaving his tax booth to follow Jesus and the Gospel tradition bearing his name.

Scripture: Matthew 9:9–13; 10:2–4; Mark 2:13–17; Acts 1:13.

St. James
09 · Apostle

St. James the Less (son of Alphaeus)

James son of Alphaeus is listed among the Twelve. He is traditionally called James the Less to distinguish him from James son of Zebedee. The New Testament gives very little personal information about him, and he should not automatically be identified with James, the brother of the Lord, without noting that this is debated.

Feast: 3 May, with St. Philip

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later accounts of his ministry and death vary and cannot be established with certainty.

Remembered for: Faithful membership among the Twelve.

Scripture: Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:16–19; Luke 6:13–16; Acts 1:13.

St. Jude
10 · Apostle

St. Jude Thaddeus

Jude, also called Thaddeus in some Gospel lists, was one of the Twelve. At the Last Supper he asked Jesus why He would reveal Himself to the disciples and not to the world. Catholic tradition honours him as a patron of difficult and desperate causes.

Feast: 28 October, with St. Simon

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Tradition associates him with missionary work and martyrdom, but the details are uncertain.

Remembered for: Hope, perseverance and apostolic faith.

Scripture: Matthew 10:2–4; Luke 6:13–16; John 14:22; Acts 1:13.

St. Simon
11 · Apostle

St. Simon the Zealot

Simon is identified as “the Zealot” in Luke and Acts and as “the Cananaean” in Matthew and Mark. He was one of the Twelve, but Scripture does not explain the precise meaning of the title or give a detailed account of his activities.

Feast: 28 October, with St. Jude

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later traditions give differing accounts of his missionary work and martyrdom.

Remembered for: His place among the Twelve and faithful witness to Christ.

Scripture: Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:16–19; Luke 6:13–16; Acts 1:13.

St. Matthias
12 · Apostle · Chosen after Judas

St. Matthias

After Judas Iscariot’s death, the disciples prayed and selected Matthias by lot to take his place among the Twelve. He had accompanied Jesus’ followers from the time of John’s baptism through the Resurrection and became a witness to the risen Lord.

Feast: 14 May in the current Roman calendar

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: The New Testament does not record his death; later traditions differ about his martyrdom.

Remembered for: Being chosen to restore the number of the Twelve and to witness to the Resurrection.

Scripture: Acts 1:15–26.

A figure in the Passion narrative

Judas Iscariot

Judas Iscariot was one of the Twelve disciples chosen by Jesus. He agreed to hand Jesus over to the chief priests for thirty pieces of silver. In the Garden of Gethsemane, he identified Jesus to the arresting group with a kiss. Matthew’s Gospel says that Judas later felt remorse, returned the silver, and went away. The New Testament records his betrayal and death, but it does not give readers a definitive statement about his final eternal destiny.

Remembered for: Betraying Jesus and the tragic warning his story gives about greed, disloyalty and despair.

Scripture: Matthew 26:14–16, 47–50; 27:3–10; Mark 14:10–11, 43–46; Luke 22:3–6, 47–48; John 13:21–30; Acts 1:16–20.

Personal reflection, not a declaration of Church teaching: “Perhaps the Lord Jesus Christ may have forgiven him — perhaps.”

Other early Christian witnesses

St. Paul, St. Barnabas, St. Titus & St. Luke

These four were important leaders and missionaries of the early Church. They are not counted among the original Twelve. Paul and Barnabas are explicitly called apostles in Acts 14:14; Titus was a trusted co-worker of Paul, and Luke is traditionally honoured as an evangelist and Gospel writer.

St. Paul
Beyond the Twelve · Apostle to the Gentiles

St. Paul the Apostle

Paul, first known as Saul, was a Jewish Pharisee from Tarsus who initially persecuted the followers of Jesus. On the road to Damascus he experienced a life-changing encounter with the risen Christ. He became a missionary to the Gentiles, helped establish Christian communities across the eastern Mediterranean, and wrote letters that became part of the New Testament. Paul was not one of the original Twelve, but the Church recognises his distinct apostolic calling.

Feast: 29 June, with St. Peter; Conversion of St. Paul — 25 January

Life: Early first century–c. AD 64–67; dates are approximate.

Death: Christian tradition holds that he was martyred in Rome under Nero; the exact date is uncertain.

Remembered for: Missionary journeys, teaching about grace and faith, and letters to the early churches.

Scripture: Acts 9:1–22; 13–28; Galatians 1:11–24; 1 Corinthians 15:8–10.

St. Barnabas
Early Church · Missionary

St. Barnabas

Barnabas, whose name is explained in Acts as “son of encouragement,” was a Levite from Cyprus. He sold property to support the community, welcomed the newly converted Saul when others feared him, and helped bring Saul to the church in Antioch. He travelled with Paul on an early missionary journey. Acts 14:14 explicitly calls both Barnabas and Paul apostles.

Feast: 11 June

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later tradition says he was martyred in Cyprus; this account is not detailed in the New Testament.

Remembered for: Encouragement, generosity, reconciliation and missionary service.

Scripture: Acts 4:36–37; 9:26–27; 11:22–26; 13–14; 14:14.

St. Titus
Early Church · Bishop and co-worker

St. Titus

Titus was a Gentile Christian and a trusted companion of Paul. Paul took him to Jerusalem as an example in the discussion about Gentile believers, later sent him to Corinth to help resolve difficulties, and entrusted him with organising the church in Crete. Christian tradition remembers Titus as the first bishop of Crete.

Feast: 26 January, with St. Timothy

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Traditionally believed to have died in Crete; the year is unknown.

Remembered for: Pastoral leadership, unity between Jewish and Gentile Christians, and faithful service.

Scripture: Galatians 2:1–3; 2 Corinthians 7:6–16; 8:16–24; Titus 1:4–5.

St. Luke
Early Church · Evangelist

St. Luke the Evangelist

Luke was a companion of Paul and is traditionally identified as the author of the Gospel according to Luke and the Acts of the Apostles. Paul refers to him as a physician and later mentions him as remaining with him. Luke’s Gospel gives particular attention to prayer, mercy, the poor, women and God’s welcome to all peoples. He was not one of the Twelve.

Feast: 18 October

Life: First century AD; exact dates unknown.

Death: Later traditions differ about his final years and death; no reliable date is known.

Remembered for: The Gospel of Luke, Acts of the Apostles and companionship in mission.

Scripture: Luke 1:1–4; Acts 1:1–3; Colossians 4:14; 2 Timothy 4:11; Philemon 1:24.

Historical note: These are traditional devotional depictions, not photographs of the people themselves. Some identities and later stories come from Christian tradition rather than direct biblical or independently verified historical evidence. Feast days can differ between Catholic and Eastern Christian calendars.
