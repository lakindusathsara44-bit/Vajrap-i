The heart of the Christian faith

JESUS CHRIST

The Eternal Word · Creator · Redeemer · Risen Lord

Christ Pantocrator icon · Byzantine Christian icon tradition. Image: Wikimedia Commons, “Christ Pantocrator icon.jpg” (public-domain reproduction). View image and source details.
Who is Jesus?

True God and True Man

In Catholic faith, Jesus Christ is not merely a prophet, teacher, or moral example. He is the eternal Son of God, the Second Person of the Holy Trinity, who became truly human for our salvation. He is one divine Person with two natures—fully divine and fully human. The Church calls this mystery the Incarnation: the Word became flesh and lived among us.

Jesus is distinct from the Father and the Holy Spirit, yet He shares the one divine nature with them. He was born of the Virgin Mary, lived a real human life, taught with authority, suffered and died, and rose bodily from the dead.

“And the Word became flesh and lived among us.”JOHN 1:14 · NRSV
Creation

Jesus Christ, the Word Through Whom All Things Were Created

The New Testament presents creation as the work of the one God, and teaches that all things came into being through the eternal Word, who became Jesus Christ. Therefore, according to Christian theology, the Son is not a created being: He is eternal, and all created things depend on Him. The Father creates through the Son in the unity of the Holy Spirit.

This is why Christians confess Jesus as Lord of all creation—not simply as someone who appeared late in history. The One who entered human history in Bethlehem is the eternal Word through whom the universe was made.

“All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being.”JOHN 1:3 · NRSV
“For in him all things in heaven and on earth were created…”COLOSSIANS 1:16 · NRSV
The Incarnation

Why Did the Son of God Become Human?

Humanity was created for communion with God, but sin wounded that relationship and brought suffering and death into human life. Out of divine love, the Son of God freely took our human nature. He entered the reality of human life, remained without sin, revealed the Father, and showed humanity the way of love, obedience, mercy, and holiness.

Jesus did not cease to be God when He became man. Nor was His humanity an appearance or disguise: He truly experienced hunger, tiredness, grief, pain, and death. In Him, divine and human natures are united in one Person without being confused or separated.

“For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son…”JOHN 3:16 · NRSV
The Cross and Redemption

He Gave His Life for the Salvation of the World

Jesus freely accepted His Passion and death on the Cross out of love for the Father and for humanity. His sacrifice is understood by the Church as the perfect and definitive offering that reconciles humanity with God. Through His obedient love, He confronted sin and death and opened the way to forgiveness and new life.

When Christians say that Jesus “paid the price” for the sins of the world, this is a theological image—not a claim that salvation is a simple financial transaction. Scripture uses several connected images: sacrifice, ransom, reconciliation, liberation, and the forgiveness of sins. At the heart of them is God's saving love revealed through Christ's self-offering.

His death was not an accident that defeated God's plan. Jesus freely offered Himself. His sacrifice is once for all, sufficient for the whole world. This gift is offered to all people; it calls for a response of faith, repentance, and life in God's grace. Catholic teaching does not mean that every person is automatically saved regardless of their response.

“He is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not for ours only but also for the sins of the whole world.”1 JOHN 2:2 · NRSV
“For there is one God; there is also one mediator between God and humankind, Christ Jesus, himself human, who gave himself a ransom for all…”1 TIMOTHY 2:5–6 · NRSV
The Resurrection

Death Did Not Have the Final Word

On the third day, Jesus rose from the dead. The Resurrection is not merely a symbol that His ideas survived: it is the central Christian confession that the crucified Jesus truly lives. By rising, He conquered death and revealed the promise of resurrection and eternal life. The Cross and Resurrection belong together: Christ gave Himself in love, and the Father raised Him from the dead.

“He is not here; for he has been raised, as he said.”MATTHEW 28:6 · NRSV
The Christian confession

Lord, Saviour, and King

Christians worship Jesus Christ as Lord. He is the promised Messiah, the Good Shepherd, the Lamb of God, the Son of Man, the Son of God, and the King whose kingdom will have no end. He continues to be present to His Church, especially in the Eucharist according to Catholic belief, and Christians await His glorious return.

The Byzantine title Pantocrator means “Ruler of All” or “Almighty.” In this icon tradition, Christ is shown as the divine Lord who blesses and teaches. The icon is not worshipped as a god; it directs the believer's attention to the person of Jesus Christ.

“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.”HEBREWS 13:8 · NRSV
