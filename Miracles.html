THE MIRACLES OF JESUS

Signs of compassion, authority, and the Kingdom of God

From the Cross to the empty tomb — and the signs of his ministry

This collection brings together the central events of the Passion and Resurrection, followed by the distinct miracle accounts recorded in the four canonical Gospels. Each entry includes a short explanation and the relevant Scripture passages.

A note on the first three entries: The Crucifixion and Jesus’ death are closely connected parts of one historical event, the Passion; they are not normally classified as miracles Jesus performed. The Resurrection is the central miracle of Christian faith. They are placed first here to follow the requested order. The Gospels do not provide one universally agreed numbered total of Jesus’ miracles: parallel accounts may describe the same event, and many healings are summarised rather than individually narrated.
BIBLICAL ACCOUNT

The Passion and victory of Christ

These three events are placed first as requested. A careful distinction matters: the Crucifixion and Jesus’ death are the saving events of the Passion, not usually counted among the public miracles he performed. The Resurrection is the central miracle of Christian faith.

ACCOUNT 01

The Crucifixion of Jesus

The Gospels describe Jesus being condemned, carrying the cross, and being crucified at Golgotha. Christians understand the Cross as the supreme sign of divine love and self-giving.

Scripture: Matthew 27:27–56 · Mark 15:16–41 · Luke 23:26–49 · John 19:16–37

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 02

The Death of Jesus

Jesus gives up his life on the Cross. The Gospel accounts describe the darkness, the tearing of the Temple curtain, and the centurion’s response. This is the completion of the Crucifixion, not a separate miracle.

Scripture: Matthew 27:45–56 · Mark 15:33–41 · Luke 23:44–49 · John 19:28–37

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 03

The Resurrection of Jesus

On the third day, the tomb is found empty and the risen Jesus appears to his followers. The Resurrection is the foundation of Christian hope and the central miracle proclaimed by the Church.

Scripture: Matthew 28:1–10 · Mark 16:1–8 · Luke 24:1–12 · John 20:1–18; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8

BIBLICAL ACCOUNT

Signs over nature and provision

These Gospel accounts show Jesus’ authority over creation and his compassion for people in need.

ACCOUNT 04

Water turned into wine at Cana

At a wedding in Cana, Jesus turns water into wine after his mother brings the need to his attention. John calls this the first of Jesus’ signs, revealing his glory.

Scripture: John 2:1–11

ACCOUNT 05

The miraculous catch of fish

After a fruitless night, Jesus tells Simon Peter to lower the nets again. The catch is so great that the nets begin to break; Peter recognises Jesus’ authority.

Scripture: Luke 5:1–11

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 06

The calming of the storm

As a storm threatens the boat, Jesus rebukes the wind and waves. The disciples are amazed that even the wind and sea obey him.

Scripture: Matthew 8:23–27 · Mark 4:35–41 · Luke 8:22–25

ACCOUNT 07

The feeding of the five thousand

With five loaves and two fish, Jesus feeds a huge crowd. The four Gospels record this sign, and the leftovers fill twelve baskets.

Scripture: Matthew 14:13–21 · Mark 6:30–44 · Luke 9:10–17 · John 6:1–15

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 08

Jesus walks on the water

Jesus approaches the disciples’ boat by walking across the lake. Matthew also records Peter stepping onto the water before becoming afraid.

Scripture: Matthew 14:22–33 · Mark 6:45–52 · John 6:16–21

ACCOUNT 09

The feeding of the four thousand

Moved by compassion for a hungry crowd, Jesus gives thanks over seven loaves and a few small fish. The crowd eats and seven baskets remain.

Scripture: Matthew 15:32–39 · Mark 8:1–10

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 10

The coin in the fish’s mouth

When the Temple tax is requested, Jesus tells Peter to catch a fish; a coin is found in its mouth to pay the tax for them both.

Scripture: Matthew 17:24–27

CONSTANTINE · SACRED ARTARTWORK · WIKIMEDIA COMMONS
ACCOUNT 11

The withered fig tree

Jesus speaks to a fig tree that has no fruit, and it withers. The Gospel uses the event to teach about faith, prayer, and the danger of fruitless appearances.

Scripture: Matthew 21:18–22 · Mark 11:12–14, 20–25

ACCOUNT 12

The second miraculous catch of fish

After the Resurrection, the disciples catch nothing until Jesus tells them where to cast the net. They bring in 153 large fish.

Scripture: John 21:1–14

BIBLICAL ACCOUNT

Healings and restoration

The Gospels describe Jesus healing illness, disability, and suffering. Parallel accounts are grouped together so the same event is not counted several times.

The official’s son healed

Jesus heals a royal official’s son at a distance. The father believes Jesus’ word, and learns that his child recovered at the very hour Jesus spoke.

Scripture: John 4:46–54

ACCOUNT 14

Peter’s mother-in-law healed

Jesus takes her by the hand and the fever leaves her. She gets up and serves those present.

Scripture: Matthew 8:14–15 · Mark 1:29–31 · Luke 4:38–39

ACCOUNT 15

A man with leprosy cleansed

Jesus touches a man with leprosy and makes him clean. He tells the man to show himself to the priest, in keeping with the Law.

Scripture: Matthew 8:1–4 · Mark 1:40–45 · Luke 5:12–16

ACCOUNT 16

The paralysed man lowered through the roof

Friends lower a paralysed man through a roof. Jesus first declares his sins forgiven, then tells him to rise, take his mat, and go home.

Scripture: Matthew 9:1–8 · Mark 2:1–12 · Luke 5:17–26

ACCOUNT 17

The man at the Pool of Bethesda

Jesus tells a man who had been ill for thirty-eight years to get up, take his mat, and walk. The man is healed.

Scripture: John 5:1–15

ACCOUNT 18

The man with the withered hand

Jesus restores a man’s hand in the synagogue on the Sabbath, challenging those who value rules over doing good.

Scripture: Matthew 12:9–14 · Mark 3:1–6 · Luke 6:6–11

ACCOUNT 19

The centurion’s servant healed

A Roman centurion trusts that Jesus can heal his servant with only a word. Jesus praises this extraordinary faith.

Scripture: Matthew 8:5–13 · Luke 7:1–10

ACCOUNT 20

The widow’s son at Nain raised

Moved by compassion for a grieving widow, Jesus tells her son to rise. The young man sits up and begins to speak.

Scripture: Luke 7:11–17

ACCOUNT 21

The woman with the haemorrhage healed

A woman who had suffered for twelve years touches Jesus’ garment in faith and is healed. Jesus speaks to her with tenderness and affirms her faith.

Scripture: Matthew 9:20–22 · Mark 5:25–34 · Luke 8:43–48

ACCOUNT 22

Jairus’ daughter raised

Jesus takes the hand of Jairus’ daughter and tells her to get up. She rises, astonishing her family and those present.

Scripture: Matthew 9:18–26 · Mark 5:21–43 · Luke 8:40–56

Two blind men receive sight

Two blind men ask Jesus for mercy. He touches their eyes, and they receive their sight.

Scripture: Matthew 9:27–31

A mute man delivered and healed

A man unable to speak is brought to Jesus. After the demon is driven out, the man speaks and the crowd is amazed.

Jesus commands a multitude of demons to leave a suffering man. The man is later found clothed and in his right mind.

Jesus responds to the persistent faith of a Gentile mother and her daughter is freed from an unclean spirit.

Jesus heals a man who is deaf and has difficulty speaking. The people proclaim that he has done all things well.

The blind man at Bethsaida

Jesus heals a blind man in stages: first his sight is partial, then he sees clearly. This account appears only in Mark.

The boy troubled by a spirit healed

Jesus rebukes the spirit tormenting a boy and restores him to his father. The story highlights the father’s plea for help and faith.

The bent woman healed

Jesus frees a woman who had been bent over for eighteen years. He calls her a daughter of Abraham and restores her dignity.

The man with dropsy healed

Jesus heals a man blind from birth. The account explores spiritual sight, testimony, and the conflict with religious authorities.

Lazarus raised from the dead

After Lazarus has been in the tomb four days, Jesus calls him out. John presents this sign as revealing Jesus as the resurrection and the life.

Deliverance and authority over evil

The Synoptic Gospels also record Jesus freeing people from unclean spirits. Some episodes overlap with healing stories above; they are not counted twice.

In the synagogue, Jesus commands an unclean spirit to leave a man. The people are astonished at his authority.

Many people freed from demons
