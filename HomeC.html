The Church in Christian history

The Holy Catholic Church

The word catholic means “universal.” In the Apostles’ and Nicene traditions, the Church is understood as the community gathered in Christ, rooted in the teaching of the apostles, the sacraments, prayer, and the proclamation of the Gospel. The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed professes belief in “one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church.”

From the earliest centuries, Christians worshipped in local churches led by bishops, preserved the apostolic teaching, celebrated the Eucharist, and cared for the poor. The ancient Christian tradition is shared in important ways by Catholics and Eastern Orthodox Christians, even as their histories, communion structures, and some theological formulations developed differently over time.

In Catholic teaching, Jesus Christ is the founder and head of the Church. The apostles were entrusted with the mission of proclaiming the Gospel, and Peter holds a distinctive role in the New Testament narratives.
